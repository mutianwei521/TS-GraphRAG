Fig. S5 | Sensor design, budget and measurement degradation affect held-out-node localization. a,b, Regional Top-1 accuracy (a) and median physical pipe-path distance from the selected training node to the true leak node (b), comparing random placement, topology maximin placement and training-response D-optimal placement across all six prespecified budgets. The filled diamond is the historical deployed 30-sensor reference, which was not newly fitted under the train-only design protocol. Points and error bars show mean ± s.d. over five prescribed Monte Carlo runs; b first calculates a median across queries within each run. c,d, Complete noise-by-sensor-availability grids for the historical reference, reporting regional accuracy (c) and the run-level median physical distance (d). Every cell shows mean ± s.d. over the same five runs. Available sensor counts are 30, 27, 22 and 15; the latter three correspond to removing rounded target fractions of 10%, 25% and 50% of 30 sensors. Removed channels are omitted from both observations and templates. Each run uses 303 training nodes and 156 queries from 78 held-out leak nodes, with independently simulated 5/15-L/s observations and 10-L/s reference templates. The baseline noise standard deviation for a,b is 0.02 m, and the historical 15-region assignment is fixed for all panels. Splits and full-channel noise fields are paired across design comparisons within a run. New response-based sensor design accesses training response rows only. All pipe distances use physical INP lengths converted to metres, not plot coordinates. Accuracy counts abstentions as misses, whereas distance summaries are conditional on a returned prediction. These are controls of a common physical retriever on one network and do not constitute retraining of the six retrieval channels plus graph aggregation. No test-set optimum or inferential significance is claimed. Source Data are provided.
