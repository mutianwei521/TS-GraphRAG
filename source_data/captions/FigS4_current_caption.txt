Fig. S4 | Partition resolution and construction change the accuracy–candidate-extent tradeoff. a, Regional Top-1 localization accuracy for pressure-weighted Leiden, topology-only Leiden and connectivity-constrained hydraulic-response Ward clustering at six prespecified region counts, K. b, Mean physical pipe length incident to the predicted region (internal plus boundary pipes, each physical pipe counted once within that region). This is a candidate-set extent proxy, not measured field effort. c, Network-wide length of cross-region boundary pipes, counting each physical pipe once. d, Assignment stability measured by the adjusted Rand index between pairs of seed-specific partitions. Points and error bars show mean ± s.d.; a–c summarize five prescribed Monte Carlo runs, each with 303 training nodes and 156 queries from 78 held-out leak nodes at 5 and 15 L/s; d summarizes the ten correlated pairwise comparisons among the five assignments and is descriptive. All designs are evaluated on one network using the same 10-L/s reference templates and physical projection-residual retriever, with 0.02-m Gaussian observation noise. Response-based design uses training perturbations only. Node predictions and physical node-distance errors are identical across partition definitions within a seed; changing region labels does not improve that invariant endpoint. Pipe lengths are read from the network model and converted to metres. Higher regional accuracy must be considered together with candidate extent. No configuration was selected on test performance; no significance test is reported. Boundary/interior sample counts and outcomes are tabulated separately. Source Data are provided.
